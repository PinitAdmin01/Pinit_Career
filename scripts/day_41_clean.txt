Day 41: JVM Throwable Hierarchy: Throwable, Error, Exception & RuntimeException
Stage 3: Systems Stress-Testing Month 2 • Block 9 • 4 Quests 🎯 Spoken Goal & Invariant: Goal: Master the exception tree: Error (JVM fatal), Exception (Checked - must handle), RuntimeException (Unchecked - programming bugs). Invariant: Checked exceptions force compile-time handling (try-catch or throws). Unchecked exceptions represent recoverable runtime bugs. ⚠️ Failure Mode & tryIt Bug: Failure: Catching java.lang.Error (like OutOfMemoryError) prevents the JVM from shutting down cleanly when resources are exhausted. tryIt Friction: Refactor "catch (Throwable t)" to "catch (Exception e)" so fatal JVM errors are not silently swallowed. 💡 3-Tier Socratic Hint Ladder: [1. Nudge: "Why is catching Throwable or Error considered dangerous in production?"] → [2. Memory: "If the JVM runs out of memory (OutOfMemoryError) or suffers StackOverflowError, catching it leaves the system in a corrupted zombie state."] → [3. Fix: "Catch specific business exceptions or at most Exception, never Throwable or Error."] 🧩 Situational Diagnostic Puzzle: "A junior developer wraps an entire microservice main loop in: "try { ... } catch (Throwable t) { log.error("Error"); }". During an OOM event, the service stays alive but drops all requests. Why?" Correct: Option B) Catching Throwable caught OutOfMemoryError, preventing the JVM from crashing and restarting, leaving it in a degraded zombie state. • Debrief: Never catch Throwable or Error. Errors indicate unrecoverable JVM conditions. Swallowing them prevents orchestrators (Kubernetes/Docker) from detecting crashes and restarting the pod.
