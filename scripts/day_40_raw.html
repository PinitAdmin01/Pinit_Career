Day 40: ⭐ Milestone 7: Pluggable Multi-Provider Payment Gateway Interface Engine

Stage 2: Defensive Fluency Month 2 • Block 8 • 4 Quests
🎯 Spoken Goal & Invariant:
Goal: Architect an enterprise pluggable payment engine supporting Stripe, PayPal, and WireTransfer via a unified PaymentProcessor interface.
Invariant: The checkout system depends strictly on the PaymentProcessor interface, remaining 100% unaware of concrete vendor classes.
⚠️ Failure Mode & tryIt Bug:
Failure: Hardcoding "new StripeProcessor()" directly inside the checkout cart couples business logic to a single vendor.
tryIt Friction: Inject PaymentProcessor interface into CheckoutService via constructor dependency injection.
💡 3-Tier Socratic Hint Ladder: [1. Nudge: "If CheckoutService writes "new StripeProcessor()", what happens when the business wants to switch to PayPal?"] → [2. Memory: "You have to modify and recompile CheckoutService! That violates the Open/Closed Principle."] → [3. Fix: "Pass PaymentProcessor into CheckoutService constructor: public CheckoutService(PaymentProcessor processor) { ... }."]
🧩 Situational Diagnostic Puzzle: "An e-commerce checkout service needs to support adding new crypto payment providers without changing a single line of existing checkout logic. What architectural principle and tool achieves this?"
Correct: Option B) The Dependency Inversion Principle using a PaymentProcessor interface and constructor injection. • Debrief: Dependency Inversion states high-level modules should not depend on low-level modules; both should depend on abstractions (interfaces). New providers plug in with zero changes to checkout logic.
