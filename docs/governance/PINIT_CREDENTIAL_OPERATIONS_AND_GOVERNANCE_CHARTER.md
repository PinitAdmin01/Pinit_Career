## 3. 👨‍⚖️ Independent Assessor Handbook & Calibration Protocol

Candidate credentialing cannot be automated by continuous integration. Credential issuance strictly requires an independent, authorized human evaluation of **Gates A through E**.

### Assessor Qualification Standards
To qualify as a PinitCareer Authorized Assessor, an evaluator must:
1. Possess a minimum of **5+ years of production fullstack or distributed systems engineering experience** in a senior, staff, or principal role.
2. Complete the **PinitCareer Assessor Calibration Course**, achieving >= 90% concordance on benchmark mock assessment evaluations.
3. Sign an annual **Conflict-of-Interest Declaration**, affirming no commercial, familial, or direct managerial relationship with evaluated candidates.

### The 5-Gate Evaluation Rubric

| Gate | Assessment Focus | Passing Threshold | Critical Failure Tripwires (Immediate Disqualification) |
|---|---|:---:|---|
| **Gate A: First Principles** | Hardware execution, event loop mechanics, POSIX signals, WAL logging, vector geometry, L2 normalization, ASCII math derivations. | >= 8.5 / 10 | Inability to explain event loop phases; claiming multi-threading in Node.js CPU loops; confusing logical storage with physical memory. |
| **Gate B: Live Incident** | Real-time troubleshooting of a live staging failure: connection pool starvation, memory leaks, RLS tenant leakage, or lock contention. | >= 8.5 / 10 | Bypassing security boundaries; hardcoding credentials; dropping tables or destroying WAL without recovery; corrupting tenant isolation. |
| **Gate C: Break-It Chaos** | Injecting multi-service cascading failures under load; diagnosing thundering herds, replica lag, and upstream timeouts. | >= 8.5 / 10 | Uncontrolled retry storms without jitter; unhandled promise rejections crashing the process; lack of circuit breaker fallback. |
| **Gate D: Novel Transfer** | Designing an end-to-end distributed system for an unfamiliar problem domain within strict SLA, latency, and cost constraints. | >= 8.5 / 10 | Hand-waving capacity math; violating CAP/PACELC trade-offs; proposing infinite horizontal scaling without persistence limits. |
| **Gate E: Grand Master Viva** | 10-prompt adversarial oral examination defending architectural trade-offs, tech stack rejections, operational costs, and lifecycle maintainability. | >= 8.5 / 10 | Defending outdated anti-patterns; unable to justify why simpler alternatives were rejected; relying on marketing claims rather than engineering telemetry. |

### Assessor Calibration & Inter-Rater Reliability
To guarantee that the credential maintains uniform labor-market value:
* **Assessor Independence & Anonymized Scoring:** To guarantee genuine evaluation independence, assessors must have zero candidate coaching relationship, zero financial interest in candidate outcome, and zero current employment or direct managerial relationship. Where operationally feasible, identity verification by Operations is formally separated from evaluation: assessors evaluate anonymized candidate code repositories and architecture packets, record independent locked scores prior to any panel discussion, and maintain an immutable audit trail for any subsequent score adjudication.
* **Multi-Rater Agreement Framework:** The program uses a pre-registered inter-rater reliability protocol appropriate for quantitative and ordinal scoring structures. Quantitative rubric scores (1–10 per gate) are evaluated using a specified two-way random-effects Intraclass Correlation Coefficient model (`ICC(2,1)` for single-assessor reliability and `ICC(2,k)` for consensus panel reliability) reporting 95% confidence intervals. Ordinal pass/fail decisions and critical-failure tripwires are evaluated using weighted agreement statistics, accompanied by Mean Absolute Difference (MAD), score variance, and pass/fail disagreement rates. No single statistical metric alone establishes credential validity.
* **Out-of-Band Immutable Records:** Every assessment record must contain:
  `candidate_id`, `assessor_id`, `assessment_timestamp`, `gate_scores`, `critical_failures`, `evidence_references`, `final_decision`, and `assessor_signature_authorization`.

## 5. 🛡️ Candidate Identity, Anti-Cheating & Appeal Governance

To safeguard the labor-market integrity of PinitCareer credentials:

1. **Candidate Identity Verification:**
   - Government-issued photo ID verification prior to scheduling high-stakes assessment gates.
   - Live biometric/visual identity confirmation at the start of oral defense sessions.
2. **Question Pool Versioning & Rotation:**
   - Oral defense prompts and live incident scenarios are drawn from an encrypted, version-controlled repository (`v1.0`, `v1.1`, etc.) rotated every 90 days to prevent rote memorization and question leakage.
3. **Retake Cooldown Policy:**
   - Candidates who fail a gate must observe a mandatory **30-day cooldown period** to engage in supervised project practicum before re-attempting the gate.
   - A maximum of **2 attempts per gate** is permitted within a 12-month window.
   - A Critical Failure on data isolation or security integrity requires repeating the associated domain practicum.
4. **Formal Appeal Procedure:**
   - Candidates may file a written appeal within **14 calendar days** of an adverse determination.
   - Appeals trigger an independent secondary review by the Chief Assessor, evaluating recorded session video, terminal logs, and submitted git commits. The secondary determination is final.
5. **Credential Revocation Policy:**
   - PinitCareer reserves the right to retroactively revoke any credential found to have been obtained through impersonation, fraudulent evidence submission, plagiary, or post-award breach of professional engineering ethics.
6. **Public Verification Infrastructure:**
   - Every issued credential exposes a permanent, cryptographically signed verification endpoint:
     `https://pinitcareer.com/verify/[credentialId]`
   - Displays candidate name, credential hash, issue date, gate verification record, and authorized assessor ID.

### Three-Phase Empirical Validation Progression
Rather than using a small calibration cohort to claim labor-market validity, PinitCareer enforces a three-phase empirical research progression:
1. **Pilot A (Assessor Calibration & Usability | 5–10 Candidates):**
   - Goal: Calibrate 3–5 assessors, eliminate rubric defects, establish timing benchmarks, and compute baseline inter-rater reliability.
   - Outputs: Mean absolute assessor differences (MAD), score variance, and rubric revisions. Zero marketing or outcome claims permitted from Pilot A.
2. **Pilot B (Reliability & Performance Distribution | 30–50+ Candidates):**
   - Goal: Evaluate psychometric consistency, gate failure distribution, critical-failure frequencies, dropout patterns, and multi-assessor consistency across candidate cohorts.
   - Outputs: Formal Inter-Rater Reliability Report with full 95% confidence intervals.
3. **Longitudinal Outcome Studies (Multiple Cohorts | N ≥ 100):**
   - Goal: Measure real-world employment rates, compensation distributions, and employer retention against matched comparison groups.
   - Outputs: Peer-reviewed labor-market outcome studies.

### Canonical 256-Bit Opaque Token & Public Anti-Enumeration Specification

Public credential verification (`/verify/[credentialToken]`) enforces minimum disclosure and strict anti-enumeration:
- **Canonical Token Model:** Exactly 32 bytes (256 bits) of cryptographically secure random entropy (`crypto.randomBytes(32)`), encoded as a 64-character lowercase hex string (`pc-cred-[64-hex-token]`). UUIDv4 is retired from public verification lookup to prevent entropy degradation.
- **Anti-Enumeration Protections:**
  1. High entropy space (2^256 possible combinations).
  2. Tiered rate limiting (IP-based and global token bucket rate limiting).
  3. Uniform, non-distinguishing `ERR_CREDENTIAL_NOT_FOUND` responses for any nonexistent, malformed, or unauthorized token.
  4. Timing equalization treated as defense-in-depth measurement.
- **Minimum Public Disclosure:** Verifier discloses only: `credentialToken`, `credentialTitle`, `issueDate`, `status` (`ACTIVE` / `REVOKED`), `issuer`, `publicKeyId`, and `signature`. Zero candidate display names, zero candidate IDs, zero candidate hashes, zero raw scorecards, zero personal candidate PII, zero telemetry.
- **Decoupled Revocation Semantics:** A cryptographically valid digital signature does not equal credential validity. If the ledger record status is `REVOKED`, `/verify` returns `REVOKED`.

## 11. 🚨 Emergency Governance Override Protocol

2. **Scope of Permissible Overrides:**
   - Granting a penalty-free session pause or rescheduling a disrupted live gate within 24 hours.

   - Authorizing forensic log reconstruction in the event of an unrecoverable telemetry capture failure.
3. **Absolute Non-Overridable Standards (The Invariant Core):**
   - 🛑 **Under no circumstances may an override lower the passing score threshold (>= 8.5 / 10.0).**

   Every granted override generates an immutable event record (`OVERRIDE_EVENT_<UUID>`) cryptographically logged to the public transparency ledger, stating the timestamp, emergency cause, affected candidate UUID, specific accommodation granted, and a 30-day automatic expiration limit.
